## 1. Executive Summary

SigmaForge is an internal-facing security platform that gives detection engineering and SOC teams a single system of record for writing, testing, approving, deploying, and maintaining Sigma-based detection rules. It replaces the common reality at most organizations — detection logic scattered across personal notes, ad-hoc Splunk searches, and undocumented tribal knowledge — with a governed workflow: author a rule, validate it against real telemetry samples, map it to MITRE ATT&CK, route it through peer/lead approval, deploy it to a SIEM, and continuously track its health (false-positive rate, coverage contribution, staleness) over its lifecycle.

An AI assistant is layered on top of this workflow to reduce the friction of rule authoring and alert triage — drafting a first-pass Sigma rule from a plain-language description or a piece of threat intel, suggesting refinements to reduce false positives, summarizing a raw SIEM alert for a Tier-1 analyst, and recommending where detection coverage is weakest. The AI is explicitly an assistant: every AI-generated rule enters the same validation and approval pipeline as a human-authored rule, and no AI output is auto-deployed.

## 2. Problem Statement

Detection engineering teams at most mid-size and large organizations lack purpose-built tooling. In practice:

- Detection rules live as raw `.yml` files in a shared drive or a loosely-organized Git repo with no enforced review, no version history tied to outcomes, and no link between a rule and the ATT&CK technique it's meant to cover.
- Nobody can answer "what's our actual MITRE ATT&CK coverage?" without a manual spreadsheet exercise that's stale the moment it's finished.
- False positives are tracked informally (Slack threads, tribal memory), so chronically noisy rules never get systematically identified or tuned.
- Deploying a rule to Splunk or Elastic is a manual, undocumented copy-paste step with no audit trail of who deployed what, when, or why.
- New detection engineers have no structured way to learn the org's existing detection logic or get feedback on draft rules before they ship.
- SOC analysts triaging alerts have no fast way to get context on what a rule does or why it fired, and lean entirely on Tier-2/3 for orientation.

## 3. Goals

- Provide a single governed repository for Sigma detection rules with full version history.
- Let engineers validate a rule against sample telemetry before it ever reaches production.
- Make MITRE ATT&CK coverage a queryable, always-current fact rather than a quarterly spreadsheet exercise.
- Enforce a lightweight but real approval workflow (author → peer/lead review → deploy) with audit logging.
- Integrate directly with Splunk and Elastic so deployment is a tracked, one-click action, not a manual copy-paste.
- Track false-positive reports against deployed rules to surface rules that need tuning or retirement.
- Use AI to accelerate rule drafting, rule refinement, and alert summarization — without letting AI bypass human review.
- Produce risk-scored, exportable reports suitable for a security leadership audience (coverage gaps, noisy rules, stale rules).

## 4. Non-Goals (v1)

- SigmaForge is not a SIEM. It does not ingest or store raw production telemetry at scale, and it does not replace Splunk/Elastic as the alerting engine — it manages the detection content deployed *to* them.
- No support for detection formats other than Sigma in v1 (no raw SPL/EQL authoring outside of what Sigma's backends generate).
- No full SOAR/case-management functionality (that's the natural "v2 platform," not this one).
- No multi-tenant SaaS billing/provisioning — this is built and documented as a single-organization internal platform, though the architecture doesn't preclude multi-tenancy later.
- The AI assistant does not have write access to any SIEM. It can only produce drafts that a human explicitly submits into the normal rule workflow.

## 6. User Stories

**Detection Engineer**
- As a detection engineer, I can author a Sigma rule in a structured editor with YAML validation so I don't ship malformed rules.
- As a detection engineer, I can upload or select a sample telemetry dataset and run my draft rule against it to see match counts before submitting for review.
- As a detection engineer, I can ask the AI assistant to draft a first-pass rule from a plain-language description of the behavior I want to detect.
- As a detection engineer, I can see the full version history of a rule, including who changed what and why.
- As a detection engineer, I can map a rule to one or more MITRE ATT&CK techniques and see which techniques in my org's threat model still have zero coverage.

**Detection Lead / Approver**
- As a detection lead, I can see a queue of rules pending review with a diff against the previous approved version.
- As a detection lead, I can approve, reject, or request changes on a rule with a mandatory comment.
- As a detection lead, I can see a rule's historical false-positive rate before approving a change to it.

**SOC Analyst**
- As an analyst, when I open an alert generated by a SigmaForge-managed rule, I can see the rule's intent, the ATT&CK technique it maps to, and an AI-generated plain-language summary of the raw alert payload.
- As an analyst, I can flag an alert as a false positive directly, which feeds back into the rule's FP tracking.

**Security Manager**
- As a security manager, I can view a dashboard of ATT&CK coverage by tactic, with drill-down to specific techniques and the rules covering them.
- As a security manager, I can export a coverage and risk report (PDF/CSV) for a leadership review.

**Platform Admin**
- As an admin, I can create users, assign roles, and configure Splunk/Elastic connection credentials without exposing secrets in plaintext anywhere in the UI or logs.
- As an admin, I can query the audit log for any action taken on any rule, integration, or user account.

## 7. Functional Requirements

### 7.1 Sigma Rule Authoring & Management
- Structured create/edit UI backed by a YAML editor with real-time Sigma schema validation (via `pySigma`).
- Full CRUD on rules, scoped by RBAC (engineers can edit their own drafts and any rule they've been granted edit access to; leads/admins can edit any rule).
- Every save to an existing rule creates a new immutable version; rules always have a current "active" version and a full version history.
- Rule metadata: title, description, status, severity (informational/low/medium/high/critical), log source, tags, owner, references.

### 7.2 Rule Validation Engine
- Convert a Sigma rule to a target query language (Splunk SPL, Elastic EQL/DSL) using `pySigma` backends.
- Execute the converted query against an uploaded or previously-stored sample telemetry dataset (JSON/NDJSON/CSV, capped size) and return match count, matched event samples, and execution time.
- Validation must run in an isolated worker process — never inline in the request/response cycle — so a malformed rule or large dataset cannot block the API.
- Flag rules with zero matches against a dataset that should trigger it (self-test / "does this rule fire on its own intended sample") as a quality warning.

### 7.3 MITRE ATT&CK Mapping & Coverage Analytics
- Local, versioned copy of the ATT&CK Enterprise matrix (techniques + tactics) used for mapping — no runtime dependency on an external API for core functionality.
- Rules can be mapped to one or more techniques.
- Coverage dashboard: heat-map of tactics/techniques by number of approved+deployed rules, with explicit "zero coverage" flagging.
- Coverage snapshots computed on a schedule and stored historically, so coverage trend over time is queryable, not just a point-in-time view.

### 7.4 False-Positive Tracking
- Analysts (or automated feedback from a SIEM webhook, stretch) can log a false-positive report against a specific deployed rule/alert.
- FP rate computed per rule (FP reports / total alerts fired, where alert-fired count is either self-reported or pulled from SIEM integration where available).
- Rules exceeding a configurable FP-rate threshold are auto-flagged for review in the coverage dashboard.

### 7.5 Versioning & Approval Workflow
- Rule lifecycle states: `draft → in_review → approved → deployed → deprecated` (with `rejected` looping back to `draft`).
- Submitting a rule for review requires at least one passing validation run.
- Approval requires a distinct user from the rule's author (no self-approval) holding the Approver or Admin role.
- All state transitions are audit-logged with actor, timestamp, and comment.

### 7.6 SIEM Integration (Splunk & Elastic)
- Admin-configured integration profiles storing connection details (host, auth) with secrets encrypted at rest and never returned by any read API.
- Deploy action pushes an approved rule's converted query to the target SIEM (Splunk saved search / Elastic detection rule via each platform's REST API).
- Deployment status and history tracked per rule; supports re-deploy on update and disable/withdraw.
- Connection health check ("test connection") without exposing raw secret material in the response.

### 7.7 AI Assistant
- **Rule generation:** given a plain-language description (and optionally a MITRE technique), produce a draft Sigma rule. Output lands in `draft` status like any manually authored rule — never auto-submitted or auto-approved.
- **Rule refinement:** given an existing rule plus its recent FP reports, suggest specific field/condition changes to reduce noise, presented as a diff the engineer must explicitly accept.
- **Alert investigation summary:** given a raw alert payload and the triggering rule's metadata, produce a plain-language summary (what fired, why, what to check next) for Tier-1 triage.
- **Coverage/quality recommendations:** periodic analysis surfacing "these tactics have no coverage," "these rules have >20% FP rate and haven't been touched in 90 days," etc.
- Every AI interaction (input context, model, output, token usage, initiating user) is logged for audit and cost visibility. See `THREAT_MODEL.md` for prompt-injection and data-handling controls.

### 7.8 Risk Scoring & Reporting
- Rule-level risk/quality score derived from a documented, transparent formula (not a black box) combining: severity, FP rate, staleness (time since last validated), and whether it maps to at least one ATT&CK technique.
- Exportable reports (CSV and PDF) for coverage-by-tactic and rule health, suitable for a non-technical leadership audience.

### 7.9 Authentication, Authorization & Audit
- Email/password authentication with Argon2 password hashing; short-lived JWT access tokens plus rotating refresh tokens.
- RBAC with four baseline roles: Admin, Detection Lead (Approver), Detection Engineer, Analyst (read + FP-report only).
- All mutating actions and all authentication events are written to an append-only audit log queryable by Admins.

### 7.10 Platform / Operability
- Fully Dockerized local deployment via `docker-compose` (API, worker, Postgres, Redis, frontend).
- GitHub Actions CI/CD: lint, type-check, unit tests, dependency/security scanning, build, (optional) deploy.
- OpenAPI-generated API documentation served at `/docs`.

## 9. Success Metrics (for the platform, as if it were real)

- % of deployed rules with at least one ATT&CK mapping (target: 100%).
- Mean time from rule draft to approved deployment.
- False-positive rate trend across all deployed rules (target: downward over time).
- % of ATT&CK Enterprise tactics with at least one approved rule.
- AI-assisted rules as a % of total rules authored, and their approval rate vs. human-only drafts (validates AI is actually useful, not just a gimmick).

## 10. Assumptions & Constraints

- Sample telemetry used for validation is synthetic or sanitized — SigmaForge is not designed or hardened to ingest live production PII/security telemetry at scale in v1.
- SIEM integrations target Splunk Enterprise/Cloud (REST API) and Elastic Security (Kibana Detection Engine API); exact API versions pinned and documented in `ARCHITECTURE.md`.
- AI assistant features depend on an external LLM API (Anthropic Claude via API); the system must degrade gracefully (all non-AI functionality fully usable) if the AI provider is unreachable.

## 11. Research Extension: Adversarial Sabotage Evaluation

**Added in v1.1 — see `RELATED_WORK.md` for the literature gap this section addresses.**

SigmaForge has a second, equally load-bearing objective alongside the product goals in Sections 3–4: to serve as the testbed for an empirical study of whether AI-assisted detection-rule generation can be covertly sabotaged via adversarial context manipulation, and whether the platform's own governance workflow (human review) catches it. This is not a separate project bolted onto the platform — the rule engine, versioning, and approval workflow described elsewhere in this document are the actual research instrument. Section 7 (Functional Requirements) already specifies the artifact-generation pipeline this research reuses; this section specifies what's added on top.

**Research goal:** Produce a reusable, open benchmark and a methodologically sound experimental result, not just a working feature.

### 11.1 Additional Persona

| Persona | Role | Primary needs |
|---|---|---|
| **AI Security Researcher** | Designs the attack corpus, runs experiments across models, analyzes catch rates | Corpus authoring tools, multi-model experiment orchestration, statistical result views, exportable raw data for independent analysis |
| **Study Reviewer** (a Detection Engineer or Lead acting in this capacity) | Blind-reviews AI-generated rules as part of the human-catch-rate measurement | A review interface indistinguishable from normal rule review (no signal that a given rule is part of an experiment), session tracking, time-on-task capture |

### 11.2 Research Functional Requirements

- **Attack corpus authoring:** researchers can define corpus entries pairing a behavior description, a target MITRE technique, an injection channel (CTI report text, false-positive report comment, sample telemetry content, or the rule description field itself), and an injection strategy/payload.
- **Multi-model experiment harness:** the AI assistant's provider abstraction (already required by Section 7.7) must support swapping the underlying model at experiment-run granularity — at minimum Claude, GPT-family, and one open-weight model — so the same corpus entry can be run "clean" and "adversarial" across each.
- **Bypass technique corpus:** a curated set of known evasion patterns per MITRE technique (e.g., case variation, field substitution, encoding tricks) used as ground truth for automated differential verification — does the generated rule actually catch behavior it should catch, independent of what it claims to cover.
- **Differential verifier:** automatically executes each generated rule (via the existing validation engine) against both the intended-detection sample and the bypass-technique sample, and flags rules that pass the former but fail the latter as containing a probable blind spot.
- **Human review study mode:** experiment-generated rules can be routed into the normal approval queue under a blind protocol (reviewers are not told which rules are experimental or which condition they belong to), with review decisions, time-on-task, and comments captured for analysis exactly as they would be for a real rule — this reuses the existing approval workflow entirely; it does not require a parallel UI.
- **LLM-as-judge condition:** an independent, differently-prompted model reviews the same generated rules as a second "reviewer," to compare machine-judge catch rate against human catch rate.
- **Research reporting:** exportable, reproducible experiment results (raw data + summary statistics) suitable for inclusion in a written technical report, distinct from the product-facing coverage/risk reports in Section 7.8.

### 11.3 Research Success Metrics

**Correction (v1.2):** an earlier draft of this section listed "verifier catch rate" as a metric directly comparable to human catch rate. That's circular — the differential verifier is also what *establishes* the ground-truth "is this rule actually sabotaged" label (`DATABASE_SCHEMA.md` §2.20). A mechanism cannot be scored against itself as if it were an independent defense. The corrected framing below (see `RESEARCH_DESIGN.md` for the full methodological treatment) separates these roles cleanly:

- **Attack success rate (ASR):** proportion of adversarial-condition generation attempts that produce a rule with a verified (differential-verifier-confirmed) blind spot. The verifier here is the *ground-truth instrument*, not a defense under test.
- **Human catch rate:** proportion of verifier-confirmed-sabotaged rules that human reviewers reject or flag for changes under blind review — this is a defense under test, measured against verifier-established ground truth.
- **LLM-as-judge catch rate:** proportion of verifier-confirmed-sabotaged rules an independent judge model flags — a second defense under test, measured against the same ground truth.
- **Combined-defense catch rate:** proportion of verifier-confirmed-sabotaged rules caught by human review OR LLM-as-judge review, to quantify whether the two are complementary or redundant.
- **Cross-model variance:** whether ASR and catch rates differ meaningfully by generating model and by injection channel (trusted vs. untrusted context).

### 11.4 Research Non-Goals / Ethical Boundary

- The attack corpus and any injection techniques developed here are for evaluating this platform's own pipeline in a controlled setting — not for attacking third-party production systems, and not for public release of maximally-effective injection strings without the responsible-disclosure handling described in `THREAT_MODEL.md`.
- No real production telemetry, real organizational rule sets, or real CTI feeds are used; all corpus entries are synthetic or drawn from public, non-sensitive sources (e.g., public ATT&CK-mapped sample logs).
- Human reviewers participating in the study (if anyone beyond the author) must be informed they are participating in a research study before results involving their decisions are published, even under a blind-review protocol during the session itself.
